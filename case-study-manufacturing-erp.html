Ongoing design project

Manufacturing Operations ERP

Company details are confidential under NDA. All visuals are conceptual recreations made for this portfolio, with fictional data. The project is in the design stage.

Project overview

A mobile ERP interface for manufacturing operations, being designed from scratch. The design work is ongoing and has not yet moved into development.

Problem context

  • Manual records and spreadsheets
  • Difficulty tracking production across multiple plants
  • Inventory and stock management challenges
  • Difficulty accessing operational reports and business information
  • Difficulty tracing some dispatch and corresponding inward records when investigating missing items or quantity mismatches
  • Some work-related entries communicated through text messages

My role

I'm responsible for designing the ERP interface in Figma: interpreting predefined requirements and screen-level expectations, organizing features into modules, identifying roles and tasks, planning workflow steps, designing mobile screens, forms and status views, building reusable components and variants with Auto Layout and consistent type, color and spacing, reviewing designs with stakeholders and developers, and refining affected screens when requirements change.

Intended user groups

AdministratorsSupervisorsPlant employeesQuality-control staffWarehouse and inventory staff

Design challenge

The workflows are interconnected. A requirement change in one process can affect several related screens and flows, and frequent changes after meetings meant iterative updates.

Design process

Requirements came from stakeholders, predefined requirements and screen details, and discussions with managers or clients. I organized features into modules, identified tasks, and designed flows from the supplied requirements. I sometimes used wireframes before creating high-fidelity UI screens.

Workflow planning

Selected design decisions

The design work covers forms, data-entry screens, tables, cards, summaries and status views, plus empty, loading, error and success states where relevant. I built these from reusable components and variants so a change can be applied consistently across screens.

Dispatch and inward traceability example

Some work-related updates were previously communicated through text messages. When dispatch and inward records weren't connected in a traceable workflow, investigating missing items or quantity mismatches could be difficult.

My design focus was to consider how dispatch and inward entries could support traceability across related operations, so it's easier to follow item movement between plants and investigate discrepancies by referring to related entries.

Intended design capability, not a verified result. This hasn't been implemented or tested.

Challenges and iterations

Requirements changed after stakeholder meetings, and I identified inconsistencies during design reviews with stakeholders and the project manager. Because workflows are connected, each change meant checking related screens and flows and updating them.

Current project status

  • Design is ongoing.
  • Not yet taken into development; no development handoff has happened.
  • Formal usability testing hasn't been conducted.
  • Outcomes can't be confirmed, and there are no impact metrics.

Key learnings

  • Understand workflows and user needs before focusing on visual polish.
  • I've become more proactive in spotting workflow gaps and suggesting changes, rather than relying only on predefined requirements.
  • It improved my problem-solving and showed me how interconnected workflows shape interface design.

Reflection

This project showed me that good interface design depends on understanding how work moves through an organization. I want to build on it by developing my UX research skills and involving users more directly in future work.
